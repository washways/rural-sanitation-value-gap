What the headline means

The headline compares recorded donor finance with estimated rural basic-sanitation capital investment need. It does not assume donors or government should pay the full household cost. The separate household-financing scenario shows how that changes the public financing requirement.

Annual investment need = unserved rural population × unit cost per person ÷ years to target
Recorded-finance coverage = annual donor/public finance ÷ annual investment need
Annual investment gap = annual investment need − recorded finance

Years to target = target year − 2026. The headline uses capital investment only; O&M and replacement are excluded. CRS 14032 is national and not rural-only, so the donor-finance share can overstate what actually reaches rural sanitation.

Potential child deaths averted

Unserved rural population = population × rural share × (1 − rural basic sanitation coverage)
Potential U5 deaths averted per year = unserved rural population × birth rate × U5 mortality × diarrhoea share × sanitation effect

National birth and under-five mortality rates are applied to the unserved rural population. The sanitation effect is applied proportionally to diarrhoeal mortality. The default 13% effect uses the Cochrane cluster-RCT estimate for diarrhoea in children under five; pooled randomized and non-randomized evidence suggests a larger effect. Other health pathways are excluded.

Rural denominator caution: World Bank rural population estimates rely on national urban/rural definitions, which are not fully harmonised across countries.

Unit cost per person

The central defaults are rounded planning values anchored to UNICEF / Hutton & Varughese regional sanitation costing. UNICEF reports average capital cost per beneficiary of about US$28 in Sub-Saharan Africa, US$13 in Central & Southern Asia and US$41 in Latin America & the Caribbean. The site applies a ±25% sensitivity around its rounded regional central value. Country costing should replace these defaults wherever available.

Mortality-only valuation

Annual mortality value = potential U5 deaths averted per year × VSL
PV mortality benefits = phased present value of each annual cohort served over a 10-year facility life at 5%
Mortality-only return per $1 = phased PV mortality benefits ÷ phased PV capital investment

Investment and benefits are phased evenly to the target year; each newly served cohort begins generating benefits when served. Because the calculation counts only under-five diarrhoeal mortality, it captures a narrow slice of sanitation benefits. It excludes morbidity, treatment costs, caregiving and productivity, nutrition and development, education, dignity, safety, convenience and environmental benefits. A return below $1 here is not a full sanitation cost-benefit result.

This is a statistical risk-valuation benchmark, not a price on an individual child.

Broader sanitation benefits

WHO states that sanitation benefits extend well beyond diarrhoea mortality, including reduced disease and malnutrition, greater dignity and safety, improved school attendance and wider economic gains. WHO's widely cited economic analysis estimated about US$5.50 in benefits for every US$1 invested in sanitation through lower health costs, greater productivity and fewer premature deaths.

UNICEF similarly links poor sanitation with morbidity, undernutrition and stunting, cognitive development, dignity, safety and economic opportunity. These benefits affect entire households, not only children. The US$5.50 figure is shown as an external global benchmark and is not multiplied into country results.

Interpretation: the mortality-only return in this tool is deliberately a floor. It should never be presented as the total sanitation return.

Financing measures

  • Basic sanitation: OECD CRS 14032, the headline measure.
  • Basic WASH: 14030 + 14031 + 14032, includes basic water supply. Context only.
  • Total WASH: sector 140, the broadest envelope. Context only.

CRS 14032 covers latrines, on-site disposal and alternative sanitation, including promotion of household and community investment. It is not coded as rural-only, so comparing it with a rural need can overstate the share reaching rural sanitation. A country with no CRS rows in any WASH sector over the period is shown as "no CRS record", not as zero.

VSL benchmark

The preferred benchmark follows the 2025 LMIC transfer function: VSL = 0.404 × (Y / 5,726)^e million US$, where Y is GNI per capita in 2020 US dollars. The central elasticity is 1.2; sensitivity uses 1.4 (conservative) and 1.0 (upper). The 2020 result is inflated to 2024 US dollars using the US CPI from the World Bank.

For Malawi, published national cost-benefit work has used a VSL range of about US$9,000–58,000. It is retained only as a historical cross-check.

No child premium is applied; the tool uses a population-average VSL.
